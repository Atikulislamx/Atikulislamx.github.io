Social Media Security Audit Checklist

A practical 30/60/90-day checklist for auditing access, admin roles, two-factor authentication and asset ownership across Meta and other social platforms.

By Atikul Islam Rabbi Last updated 13 October 2026

Most social media incidents are not sophisticated. They are an old employee who still had admin rights, a shared password in a spreadsheet, two-factor authentication switched off on the one profile that controls everything, or an agency that was never removed. An audit finds those before an attacker does.

The inventory (start here)

You cannot secure what you have not listed. Build one table with a row per asset:

  • Facebook Pages, Instagram accounts, ad accounts, pixels, catalogs and domains.
  • The Business Manager that owns each asset, and its business ID.
  • Every person with access, their role, and why they have it.
  • Recovery email and phone for each personal profile that holds admin.
  • Connected third-party apps and scheduling tools.

If a single personal profile appears as the only admin for most assets, that is your biggest finding on day one.

Access and roles

  • Minimum two admins per business asset, on separate people.
  • No shared logins; agency access granted through Business Manager, never by password.
  • Former team members removed on the day they leave, with a written check.
  • Quarterly review of Business settings → People and connected apps.

Authentication

  • App-based two-factor or a passkey on every admin profile — SMS is the weakest option and is vulnerable to SIM swap.
  • Recovery email on a domain the business controls.
  • Trusted contacts configured, so a lockout is survivable.
  • No one-time codes sent over chat, ever.
The most common breach path in small businesses is not malware. It is a message that says "I'm from Meta support, send the code". Train the person who answers the inbox.

Asset ownership

  • Every Page and ad account sits inside a Business Manager owned by the business, not by an agency or a freelancer.
  • Business verification completed, with documents stored somewhere the company controls.
  • Domains verified in Business Manager; unknown verification tokens removed.
  • Pixel and catalog ownership confirmed, with no legacy tokens from former vendors.

Monitoring and response

  • Login alerts enabled and routed to a monitored inbox.
  • A one-page incident plan: who calls the bank, who contacts Meta, who speaks to the audience, who keeps the evidence log.
  • An offline copy of the asset inventory and key documents.

30 / 60 / 90 days

WindowOutcome
Days 1–30Inventory complete, unknown admins removed, 2FA enforced on every admin profile
Days 31–60Ownership consolidated into the company Business Manager, verification filed, domain and pixel ownership confirmed
Days 61–90Offboarding process documented, incident plan tested, quarterly review scheduled

A structured audit compresses this into a prioritised report — what is exposed now, what it would take to fix, and what to do first. If you would rather start with a conversation, describe your setup.
