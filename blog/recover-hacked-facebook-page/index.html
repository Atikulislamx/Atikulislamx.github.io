How to Recover a Hacked Facebook Page

A structured process for taking back a Facebook Page after admin access is removed: what to document, which Meta routes to use, and what makes a case fail.

By Atikul Islam Rabbi Last updated 18 November 2026

A Page takeover is rarely a single event. Access is usually lost in one of three ways: an admin is removed by someone who still holds a role, the personal profile behind the admin role is itself compromised, or the Page is absorbed into a Business Manager that the attacker now controls. The route back is different in each case, which is why "just use the recovery form" fails so often.

First rule: do not delete the Page, do not create a duplicate, and do not pay anyone who guarantees recovery. Both actions make a legitimate claim harder to prove.

1. Establish what actually happened

Before contacting Meta, write down the sequence of events. A support team, and a reviewer, needs a timeline that can be checked:

  • The last time you had working admin access, with a date.
  • Any email or notification about an admin change, a login from a new device, or a policy notice.
  • Whether the Page is linked to a Business Manager, and who is listed as its owner.
  • Whether the email address or phone number on your personal profile was changed.

If your personal profile still works but the Page is gone from your list, the problem is a role change. If your profile is also locked out, the problem starts there — account recovery comes first, because every Page route depends on a profile that Meta can verify.

2. Check Business Manager before the Page

Most high-value Pages are attached to a Business Manager. Open Business settings → Accounts → Pages and look at who holds admin or full control. If an unknown person appears, remove them first, then claim the Page back into your own portfolio. If you cannot open that Business Manager at all, you are dealing with a Business Manager takeover, which needs its own claim.

3. Use the recovery routes in the right order

  1. Page roles route. From the Page's help menu, request admin access and complete identity verification. This works when ownership evidence still points to you.
  2. Account recovery route. If the admin profile is compromised, restore the profile using the identity documents attached to that account, then return to the Page.
  3. Business Manager ownership route. Where a Business Manager holds the Page, the claim is filed against the business, not the Page. Proof of legal entity matters more than a profile here.
  4. Support inbox escalation. Only after a case exists — an escalation without a case number and a clean evidence set tends to be closed as duplicate.

4. Build the evidence file

Reviewers decide on documents, not explanations. A strong file contains:

  • Government ID matching the name on the admin profile (never sent over chat or a form to a third party).
  • Business registration or a tax document matching the Page's name.
  • Older admin invitations, screenshots of the Page's dashboard from before the loss, or Meta emails addressed to you.
  • Domain or website control, if the Page represents a business with a site.

Redact everything that is not needed. A reviewer does not need your home address, payment details, or audience data.

5. What makes a case fail

  • Conflicting claims. Two people filing for the same Page slows both.
  • Mismatched identity. The name on the ID, the profile, and the business documents do not agree.
  • Unresolved policy strikes. A Page under a content or trademark restriction is reviewed differently from one that was only hijacked.
  • Repeated duplicate tickets. Each new ticket without a case number resets the context.

6. Secure the Page the moment it returns

Recovery without hardening invites a second takeover. Within the first hour: remove every unrecognised admin, switch on two-factor authentication for all remaining admins, review connected apps and business integrations, confirm the recovery email and phone, and check scheduled posts and payment methods before they are used.

Realistic expectations

Some recoveries close in days. Others take weeks of review, and some are not recoverable — for example where the Page was deleted, or where ownership genuinely cannot be evidenced. A professional assessment should tell you which of those you are looking at before any work starts. If you want that review, describe the situation and I will tell you plainly what is realistic.
